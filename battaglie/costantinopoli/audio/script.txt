For a thousand years, no army broke these walls.
Then a twenty-one-year-old sultan brought a cannon nine meters long.
Constantinople, 1453. Water on three sides.
On the fourth, the Theodosian Walls: a moat, an outer wall, and an inner wall twelve meters high, with ninety-six towers.
Sultan Mehmed the Second came with around eighty thousand men.
Emperor Constantine the Eleventh had about seven thousand.
But Mehmed had Orban's cannon. Its stone balls weighed half a ton.
It fired only a few times a day... and every hit tore the wall open.
Each night, the defenders rebuilt the breach.
The harbor was sealed by a giant chain.
So Mehmed did the impossible.
He dragged seventy ships over a hill, on greased logs, into the Golden Horn.
Now the city was under attack from every side.
On May twenty-ninth, after fifty-three days, the wall finally broke.
Constantine was last seen fighting in the breach.
An empire that had lasted a thousand years fell to a cannon... and a fleet that crossed a hill.
